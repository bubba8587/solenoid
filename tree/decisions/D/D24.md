## Decision

**MUST:** a routed function declares how its arguments' nulls and errors are prepared. There are five policies:

- **`RANGE_RAW`**: cells untouched. The COUNT family, which classifies cells rather than computing with them.
- **`RANGE_POSITIONAL`**: positions preserved. Lookups and index functions.
- **`RANGE_ZERO_FILL`**: nulls become 0 in place. `NPV` and `SERIESSUM`, whose arguments are periods, so dropping one would shift every later one.
- **`RANGE_PAIRED`**: pairs are dropped together by index. Term-by-term functions.
- **The default, pooled**: nulls dropped from each array independently. Aggregators.

Whole-list natives that preserve positions skip `prepRangeArgs` entirely, through `takesWholeArgs`.

## Why

No single policy is right for every function. The aggregator policy breaks a positional function: `REVERSE([1, null, 3])` must be `[3, null, 1]`, never `[3, 1]`. The paired policy breaks two independent samples of different lengths.

## Consequences

- *Exception:* `T.TEST` and `F.TEST` use the pooled policy even though they compare two arrays. Their arrays are samples that may differ in length for an independent test, and the paired policy's shortest-length zip would throw away the tail of the longer one. **Removed by:** routing per test type, if the evaluator ever dispatches on an argument's value.
