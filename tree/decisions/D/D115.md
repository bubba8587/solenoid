## Decision
- A date plus or minus a number is a date, the number read as days.
- A number plus a date is a date.
- A date minus a date is a plain number of days.
- Every other arithmetic on a date is #TYPE!: adding two dates, multiplying, dividing, negating, raising to a power, a sum or product of two or more dates, a share of dates.
- Two dates compare with each other. A date compared with a number or text is #TYPE!.

## Why
A date is a position on the calendar, not an amount: you can move it and measure between two of them, but adding or scaling one means nothing. Solenoid holds the same rule for temperature readings ([[C25]] firstClassUnits), so the two agree.

## Consequences
- This breaks from Excel, where a date is a number and `date * 2` answers. Each divergence goes in the Excel divergence list (today `formulajs-divergences.md`, to be renamed `excel-divergences.md` now that Formula.js is ported away).
- **Reopen if:** a date minus a date should be a duration with a unit (days, as a unit value) instead of a plain number.
