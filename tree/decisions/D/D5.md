## Decision

**MUST:** a catalog entry's visible `label` carries only what a reader needs to identify and pick it. Every alternate spelling a user might type, Excel function names first, is declared in a field that search reads but the menu never shows (`keywords`, or `CATALOG_TO_EXCEL` for a whole leaf). It is never appended to the label to make the entry findable.

## Why

Being findable and being readable are separate jobs, and the search layer already keeps them apart: `scoreLeaf` scores `keywords` at full weight, so moving a name out of the label costs nothing and can improve its rank. A stuffed label pays twice. The name is duplicated against the declaration it came from ([[C8]] declareOnce), and because the Add menu sizes every row to the widest one, one overlong label spoils the whole menu. When the Distribution ops each carried their full dotted Excel list, one row measured 630px against a 94px median and stretched the panel from 174px to 525px on the first keystroke.

## Consequences

- The search mechanism is in [[add-menu]].
- *Exception:* a parenthetical that tells apart two entries sharing a name stays in the label ("T.TEST (equal var)" and "T.TEST (Welch)", "TAKE (table)", "DATE (Build)"), and so does a bare acronym that is the name people know ("Growth Rate (CAGR)"). What is barred is an Excel function spelling: a dotted one, or several separated by slashes. **Removed by:** the menu gaining per-row truncation. The length pressure would then be gone, and only [[C8]] declareOnce would still argue against it.
