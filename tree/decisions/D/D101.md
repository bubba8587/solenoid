## Decision

A finance function's money arguments (present and future value, payment, cash flows, cost, par) are in one currency. A result that is money comes out in that currency, a rate or a count of periods comes out as a plain number, and rates and period counts are plain numbers going in. A plain number where money goes reads in the money's currency. The cards and the formulas answer alike.

## Why

A payment on a dollar loan is dollars ([[C25]] firstClassUnits). Refusing it leaves a money column with no way into PMT, and dropping the unit is the silent loss C25 forbids. The time-value equation these functions solve gives this answer through ordinary arithmetic, as the TVM card already shows.

## Consequences

- A rate or period count with a time unit (5%/yr, 30 yr) is #UNIT!: turning an annual rate into a period rate takes a convention (nominal or effective) the inputs don't state.
- Two currencies are #UNIT! ([[D47]] noMixCurrencies).
- **Reopen if:** Solenoid gains exchange rates, or a convention for rates per unit of time.
