## Decision

**MUST:** a computed number never leaves its producing op as a bare `NaN` or `±Infinity`. The producer classifies it through `guardFinite` (`valueKinds.ts`): `NaN` becomes `#DOMAIN!`; `±Inf` from all-finite inputs becomes `#OVERFLOW!`; `±Inf` when an input was already infinite passes, since the Constant node's ∞ is a real value. The guarded producers are the element-wise broadcasters (`shared.ts`), the formula operators (`applyOp`, unary, percent), `broadcastCall`, the RANGE dispatch, the frame aggregation path (`guardAgg`, frameVerbs) and the native engine's result normalizer (frameBackend). A kernel with its own recorded non-finite convention (a quiet null, a tagged error, IMDIV's `cx(NaN, NaN)`) is the deliberate alternative, not an exemption from deciding.

## Why

A bare NaN renders as an empty cell and computes onward as more NaN: a wrong answer that doesn't look like one, the least visible failure in the model. The RANGE branch was the last leak. It returned dispatch results raw, unlike `broadcastCall`, so nine whole-sample calls (STDEV of one value, CORREL of a constant, GEOMEAN of a negative, and degenerate SLOPE, RSQ, SKEW, KURT, VAR and Z.TEST) answered bare NaN.

## Consequences

- A non-finite number is a key of its own in Distinct, GROUPBY and Window partitions: `+∞`, `−∞` and `NaN` are three keys, not one. Sort already puts `±∞` at opposite ends and aggregation reads `NaN` as `#DOMAIN!` while passing `±∞`, so one shared bucket would merge values every other surface tells apart.
- A non-finite join key never matches, not even another of its kind. `NaN` does not equal itself, and `±∞` marks an overflow, so two rows that both overflowed are not the same entity. Both engines mask such keys to unmatchable.
