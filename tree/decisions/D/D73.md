## Decision

**MUST (the author's standing order):** a node can do everything the formula it shares a name with can. Every argument, mode and result shape the formula reaches has a socket, op or control on the node; the node never offers less and leaves the rest to the formula bar.

## Why

A node that reaches less than its formula sends the user to the formula bar for the rest, and the node stops being a complete answer to "what does this function do". Sharing one implementation ([[C17]] shareImpl) keeps the two surfaces' answers equal but says nothing about their reach: a node that forwards three of a function's four arguments shares the code and still can't do what the formula does.

## Consequences

- A node may lack an argument when there's a recorded reason: the argument only makes sense in a cell grid, or the formula surface lacks it too.
- **Reopen if:** a function's reach can't be shown on a node without making it unreadable; the reason is then recorded against that function.
- How it's built: the node-classes floor spec.
