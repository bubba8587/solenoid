## Decision

**MUST (the author's standing order):** a node can do everything the formula it shares a name with can. Every argument, mode and result shape the formula reaches has a socket, op or control on the node.

- A node that dispatches through `resolveExcelFunction` passes the function's full argument list.
- A node with its own computation covers every mode the registration does, preferably by routing both surfaces through one shared kernel ([[C17]] shareImpl). The REGEX ops, for example, compose `regexApply`, `replaceNth` and `regexGroups` on both sides.

## Why

A node that reaches less than its formula sends the user to the formula bar for the rest, and the card stops being a complete answer to "what does this function do". Sharing one implementation ([[C17]]) keeps the two surfaces' answers equal but says nothing about their reach: a node that forwards three of a function's four arguments shares the kernel and still can't do what the formula does.

## Consequences

- A node may lack an argument when there's a recorded reason: the argument is a cell-grid concept, or the formula surface lacks it too.
- **Reopen if:** a function's reach can't be shown on a card without making it illegible; the reason is then recorded against that function.
