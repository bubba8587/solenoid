## Decision

A calculation that mixes two different temperature units, such as K with °C or °C with °F, answers #UNIT! and asks for a conversion first. That includes a K difference added back to a °C reading: `20 °C + 5 K` is #UNIT!, while `20 °C + 5` works as before, and `25 °C - 20 °C` gives 5 Δ°C, a difference that adds back to a °C reading.

## Why

Kelvin is written both for a temperature and for a change in temperature, and nothing in the value says which, so any answer would be a guess. Refusing is plain and easy to fix; a wrong guess is a believable wrong number.

## Consequences

- How it's built: [[unit-flow]].
- *Unenforced:* the Arithmetic card treats a K value as a change (`20 °C + 300 K` gives 320 °C), and formulas have no rule for mixing (`docs/backlog.md` § Leaves the code breaks).
- **Reopen if:** people need to mix them; the choices then are K as a reading next to °C or °F, or K as a change, with a rule for a difference added back to a reading.
- 2026-10-29 The subtraction example now gives Δ°C, matching the sibling leaf on temperature differences. The rule is unchanged. By Claude Opus 5.5.
