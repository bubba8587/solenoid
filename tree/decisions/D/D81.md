## Decision

In a computed column over a Cube, `@name` on a column of lists reads this row's list, and the formula may answer a list, which becomes that row's cell. Read whole, a column of lists is its rows stacked and padded with blanks, so `SUM(prices)` totals every row's items. A nested table stays out of formulas.

## Why

In a Cube a cell can hold a list, and formulas take lists, so `@name` reads it; refusing would leave a Cube's list columns unreachable from the one place per-row math lives. A Cube row can hold a list, so the answer can be one too.

## Consequences

- `SPARKLINE(@history)` in a Cube's formula column draws each row's own list.
- A Frame row can't hold a list, so a Frame's computed column still answers one value per row.
- How it's built: [[computed-columns]].

## Contest

### Contest 2026-09-29 by Claude Opus 5.5: keep wins

Keep wins against C22 and C15: @name reading this row's list is C22's @ on a cell that holds a list, which C15 lets into a formula. Opposite (no list cells in formulas) makes a Cube's list columns unreachable. Deletion leaves the Cube surface unspecified. The whole-column clause is amended on the author's word: a column of list cells reads as its rows stacked, padded with blanks.
