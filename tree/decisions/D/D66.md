## Decision

The engine has two precision modes, **Days** and **Minutes**, chosen on the card. It never uses one granularity with a display-only snap.

- **Days** schedules in whole working days. A finish-to-start (FS) link with lag 0 starts the successor on the next working day, and `Finish - Start + 1 = Duration`.
- **Minutes** schedules in Microsoft Project's working intervals (08:00 to 17:00), so an FS successor can start the same afternoon.

In both modes a task's Finish is inclusive of the day in its cell. In Minutes mode a finish at midnight displays on the previous day.

## Why

A display-only snap shows two tasks on the same day and reads as an overlap, and snapping inside a single engine breaks MSPDI parity on durations given in hours. Two honest modes cost less than one dishonest one. These are precision modes of the whole engine, not the per-task constraint modes that [[C70]] oneScheduleRule forbids, and the four columns keep one rule each in both.

## Consequences

- The mode is part of what the plan means, so it persists with the document and travels in the figure payload.
- **Reopen if:** a third precision (shifts, seconds) is asked for. It would be a third mode, not a snap.
