## Decision
A card's result has the type of what it computed, and no setting declares it. Where a card offers a type pick on its result, the pick is an InlineCast: a Cast labelled as one, sitting on the result, running Cast's own conversion. Its first option is ∅, no cast, and that is the default. Toggles that say how an input is read (Value Input's type, Get Column's Read as) are not InlineCasts and stay separate.
## Why
After the calculation the type is known ([[C136]] typesKnownAfterCalc): a date comes from what the formula does with dates, text and true/false carry their kind, and everything else is a number. A picker that only relabels the result would claim a type the value doesn't have, and one that converts on its own would be a second, hidden Cast ([[C137]] datesNeverSerials: only Cast turns a date into a number). Casting right on the result still saves a card, so the pick stays, named for what it is.
## Consequences
- The Formula card and the LAMBDA helpers (MAP, BYROW / BYCOL, REDUCE, SCAN, MAKEARRAY) carry an InlineCast in place of their result-type toggle.
- An InlineCast converts exactly as the Cast card does, cell by cell, with its errors: a date cast to Number gives the serials, as Cast does.
- An answer that mixes kinds is typed as mixed, and an empty one takes the type its formula and inputs give; neither takes a pick's word for it.
- **Reopen if:** a card's computed type turns out to be unknowable after the run, so a declaration is needed after all.
