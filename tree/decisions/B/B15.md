## Decision

Solenoid ships a lean core, and most node families are optional packs that can be switched on or off. The shared toolkit every pack builds on is always in the core, never an add-on: the canvas and engine, units, socket types and their legend, the formula language, the basic look of a node, and saving and loading.

## Why

Someone opening Solenoid for the first time should see a clean, simple tool, not two hundred specialist nodes they'll never use. Keeping the toolkit in the core means packs all build on the same ground, so their graphs fit together.

## Consequences

- **Reopen if:** packs need to ship their own units or socket types.

## Alternatives considered

### Contest 2026-09-15 by Claude Opus 5: keep wins

Rubric: [[A1]].
- keep: lean core, optional packs, a fixed shared toolkit. Cost: pack registry, activation store, a formula-pack format; the toolkit is frozen as a promise.
- opposite: every node family in the core, always visible. The Add menu and Reference grow to hundreds of specialist nodes; the scratchpad end of [[A1]] drowns.
- deletion: [[A1]] alone does not say where a specialist node family belongs or what packs may rely on; each new family re-argues it.
- variant: packs may ship their own units or sockets. Two packs disagreeing on units breaks wiring between them, and the flagship unit feature fractures.
Verdict: keep.
