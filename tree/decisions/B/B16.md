## Decision

The formula surface and the node canvas share one function set. A function has the same name on both, one implementation behind both, and the same answer. Parity is measured against current Excel, on every surface at once (the formula bar, the Add menu, the Function Reference, catalog descriptions). Where an Excel quirk would make the two engines disagree, consistency wins and the divergence is named. Nodes that no formula can express, such as the Frame verbs, figures and inputs, aren't functions and sit outside this rule.

## Why

[[A5]] excelParity is the promise, and this is the shape that keeps it. Two implementations of one function drift, and a name that differs between the formula bar and the card is a trap of the same kind as a wrong answer. Measuring against current Excel, not 2003's aliases, lets a user's knowledge carry over without a legacy layer, and naming every divergence is what lets [[A5]] and [[A6]] divergeByDesign coexist.

## Consequences

- The families under this node: one implementation ([[C17]] shareImpl); naming ([[C51]] formulaNaming, [[C18]] uniqueNameMap, [[C14]] currentExcelParity); arguments ([[C20]] wholeArrayArgs, [[C21]] matchNodeLimits, [[C22]] rowFormulaRefs, [[C80]] blankArgIsExcelBlank, [[C15]] matricesInFormulas, [[C50]] lambdaBindsByName); answers ([[C45]] excelComparisons, [[C46]] consistencyOverQuirks); and recalculation ([[C23]] calcModes).
- A new function lands on both surfaces in one change, or on neither.
- The named divergences live in two lists: differences from Excel itself in [[formula-language]] (*Deliberate differences from Excel*), and overrides of Formula.js in [[formulajs-divergences]].
- **Reopen if:** a surface is wanted that deliberately offers fewer functions, such as a simple mode. It would be a filter over the one set, never a second set.
