## Decision

A value on a cable is typed and self-describing, and its type is part of its meaning. A socket is a family at a rank; nulls and per-cell errors are first-class; a unit belongs to the value; a date is a real serial; a figure is a value like any other; and a column, not a cell, is where a definition lives. Nothing coerces silently: a connection that would change what a value means is refused, or made explicit with a node the user can see.

## Why

[[A6]] divergeByDesign names Excel's silent coercion, its blindness to units and its per-cell formulas as the frustrations the product exists to fix. They are one fix, not several: each is a place where Excel lets a value mean less than it says. A calculator people can trust carries what a value is along with what it holds, and every family under this node applies that promise to one kind of value.

## Consequences

- The families under this node: the socket lattice ([[C10]] socketLattice), nulls and errors ([[C24]] arraySemantics), units ([[C25]] firstClassUnits), dates ([[C44]] dateSerials), figures ([[C100]] chartIsAValue), the column as the definition ([[C54]] noPerCellFormulas), the one typed escape hatch ([[C66]] scriptNode), and a fill that answers null rather than guess ([[C102]] gridFillThenForecast).
- Every new node is type-honest and unit-honest. A node that can't say what it emits emits a wildcard and adopts.
