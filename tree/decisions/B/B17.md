## Decision

A value on a cable knows what kind of thing it is, and that is part of its meaning. Text, numbers, dates and true/false are different kinds, and a single value, a list and a table are different shapes. A blank and an error are values in their own right. A unit belongs to its number. A date is a real date, counted in days the way Excel counts them, and never mistaken for a plain number. A chart is a value like any other. A formula belongs to a column, not to each cell. Nothing is converted behind the user's back: a connection that would change what a value means is refused, or made with a node the user can see.

## Why

[[A6]] divergeByDesign names Excel's silent conversions, its blindness to units and its formula in every cell as the frustrations the product exists to fix. They are one fix, not several: each is a place where Excel lets a value mean less than it says. A calculator people can trust carries what a value is along with what it holds, and each leaf under this one applies that promise to one kind of value.

## Consequences

- The leaves under this one: which connections are allowed ([[C10]] socketLattice), blanks and errors ([[C24]] arraySemantics), units ([[C25]] firstClassUnits), dates ([[C44]] dateSerials), charts ([[C100]] chartIsAValue), the column as the place a formula lives ([[C54]] noPerCellFormulas), the one node where the user writes their own code ([[C66]] scriptNode), and a LAMBDA wired by name rather than by position ([[C50]] lambdaBindsByName).
- Every new node is honest about what kind of value it gives and what unit it carries. A node that can't know its output in advance says so, and takes on the kind of whatever is wired to it.
