## Decision

A value on a cable knows what kind of thing it is, and that is part of its meaning: a number, text, a date or true/false; a single value, a list or a table; a blank or an error; and its unit, if it has one. Nothing converts a value behind the user's back: a connection that would change what a value means is refused, or made with a node the user can see.

## Why

Solenoid makes types and units first-class where Excel lets them slide ([[A6]] divergeByDesign), so a value has to carry what it is along with what it holds, and nothing may quietly change it.

## Consequences

- Every new node is honest about what kind of value it gives and what unit it carries. A node that can't know its output in advance says so, and takes on the kind of whatever is wired to it.
- **Reopen if:** Solenoid stops treating types and units as first-class.
