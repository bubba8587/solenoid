## Decision

A formula the date rule would refuse is judged as a whole by its point weight, as a temperature reading's is: with the dates it is given as the points, a formula of weight 1 answers a date and one of weight 0 a number of days, so (d1 + d2) / 2 and 0.25·d1 + 0.75·d2 are dates. Anything else stays refused: d1 + d2 alone, d1 * 2, a third of a sum.

## Why

The midpoint of two dates is a real date wherever the calendar starts, and Excel already answers it; the date rule refused it only because it judged each operator alone, which also broke its promise that dates and temperature readings agree.

## Consequences

- Only formulas the operators refuse take this path, so no formula that answered before changes.
- A refused piece is judged on its own too, so YEAR((d1 + d2) / 2) is the midpoint's year and (d1 + d2) / 2 > d3 compares a date. A date the formula makes itself (DATE, TODAY) added to another date stays refused.
- The Arithmetic card still refuses adding two dates: it sees one operation, not the formula.
- Removed by: dates becoming a point kind the evaluator carries weight for directly.
