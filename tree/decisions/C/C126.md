## Decision

When a Decision Matrix ranks a Cube, each nested Frame is a further choice under its row: it is ranked with the same Weights Frame, and the row counts the best option inside it as one more criterion, weighted by the nested column's name. A row with nothing acceptable inside it is ruled out. The ranking keeps every nested Frame, ranked, and names each row's best option, so the first choice (a neighborhood) already accounts for the best second choice it opens (its best apartment), to any depth.

## Why

Choices come in chains: a city, then a neighborhood, then an apartment. Judging the neighborhood on its own criteria picks one whose apartments are all poor, and judging apartments alone ignores the neighborhood. Scoring each step with the best it leads to is how a person reasons backward from the end, and with weighted scores it needs nothing beyond the same matrix at each level.

## Consequences

- How it's built: [[frame-verbs#Decision Matrix and Decision Sensitivity]].
- Choices made in the face of chance, where what happens after the first choice isn't known, are not this; they need probabilities.
- **Reopen if:** a chain needs its second step's choice to weigh differently from its own criteria (a separate Weights Frame per level).
