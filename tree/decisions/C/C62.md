## Decision

A palette can replace App.css's neutral ramp: 13 keys per theme mode (the window, the canvas and its dot, three surfaces, three borders and four inks), held in `BUILTIN_CHROME`. The ramp sits beside the slot map rather than adding slots to it. The other neutrals App.css writes as literal values (panel and overlay fills, the overlay border, button hover, the gauge track, the selected cable, the wordmark and the light-theme shadows) derive from those 13 by fixed mixes.

A palette authors all of the ramp or none of it. `Default` authors none, because it is App.css; every other built-in authors a complete ramp in both modes. A ramp may recolor the workbench but may not break its structure (listed under Consequences). A tinted ramp follows the live accent. The contrast requirement (WCAG AA, 4.5:1 for ink on card and field) applies to two palettes only: `Default` and `Colorblind-safe`.

## Why

Nothing stores a neutral on a node and `resolveColor` never returns one, so folding the neutrals into the slot map would force every slot consumer (chart series, the swatch grid, the height ramp, document overrides) to skip them. The derived neutrals were calibrated by running the Default ramp through the mixes and matching App.css's own hand-tuned literals. An author therefore moves one ramp, and a mix that reproduces the tuned values also behaves on a ramp nobody has checked by eye.

A partial ramp would write its own keys and derive nothing, leaving the app in a mix of authored neutrals and stylesheet ones, and a copy of Default would be two homes for one truth. Tinted ramps rotate in OKLCh because the first version rotated in HSL, whose saturation varies with hue: the workbench came out "washed in the color" (the author's words), at up to twice the authored perceived chroma on Orchard's dark ground. Contrast is limited to two palettes because Default is the look nobody chose and Colorblind-safe exists for legibility, while the rest are aesthetic opt-ins whose value is fidelity to a look (Solarized sits near 3:1 by design).

## Consequences

- `appTheme` clears every chrome variable on each apply, because an inline property beats the stylesheet and a stale one would strand cream chrome under Default. The Custom palette's ramp is always complete, seeded from `DEFAULT_CHROME`, a hand-kept mirror of App.css.
- Structure holds for every ramp, the App.css baseline included (a rule that Default fails is a wrong rule): the canvas is darker than a card; the dot is legible on the canvas without shouting; the field is the brightest surface in light mode and a recess in dark mode; the hover fill steps toward the ink; the three border tiers step outward; the four ink tiers step down in contrast.
- Tinted ramps: Orchard and Blueprint declare a home accent (`CHROME_HOME`: green and blue). `appTheme` rotates the whole ramp in OKLCh by the hue difference to the selected accent, holding each key's chroma and its WCAG relative luminance, so at the home accent the authored ramp comes back byte-identical. Chroma may only drop in transit, never grow. This does not reopen the socket-sibling HSV rule (DESIGN.md §Tertiary), whose steps stay near their own hue. Each palette declares whether it adapts: Solarized, Colorblind-safe, Equinox, Muted and Custom do not rotate. An accent below the OKLCh chroma floor leaves the ramp as authored.
- **Reopen if:** chrome outgrows flat colors (a texture, a vignette, a per-document ramp), which would need its own model. If a palette other than these two ever becomes anyone's default, it joins `AA_PALETTES` (`palette.test.ts`) that day.
