## Decision

A palette either sets every neutral color of the workbench around the graph (the window, the canvas and its dots, the surfaces, borders and text, in light and dark) or sets none of them and keeps the app's own. The other neutrals are mixed from that one set. A palette may recolor the workbench but never break its structure, a tinted palette's workbench follows the accent the user picked, and only Default and Colorblind-safe must meet the contrast standard for text.

## Why

A palette that set only some of the neutrals would leave the app wearing two looks at once, and mixing the rest from one set means a whole palette moves together. Default and Colorblind-safe must be easy to read because one is what nobody chose and the other exists to be legible; the rest are looks people opt into.

## Consequences

- A document pinned to a palette takes its workbench colors too, so it looks the same wherever it opens.
- How it's built: [[palette-and-theme#The neutral chrome ramp]].
- **Reopen if:** the workbench needs more than flat colors, such as a texture, a vignette or a workbench per document.
