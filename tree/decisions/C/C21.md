## Decision

**MUST:** a formula function that generates values (SEQUENCE, RANDARRAY, MAKEARRAY and the like) stops at the same size limit as the matching node, and answers `#OVERFLOW!` past it.

## Why

[[B16]] oneFormulaSurface makes the formula and the node one function, so they keep one limit rather than the formula quietly allowing more. On a node, the count is a spinner the user watches. In a formula field, one typo can ask for ten million elements with nothing on screen to stop it.

## Consequences

- The limit and the functions it covers: [[formula-language]].

## Contest

### Contest 2026-09-26 by claude: keep wins

keep: one function on two surfaces (B16) means one limit; a formula outgrowing its node is a second answer. opposite (no formula limit) makes the formula surface do what the node refuses. deletion leaves the limit per surface. Keep.
