## Decision

Inside a composite you work on the same canvas as the main one: gestures, the lasso, menus and keys all behave the same. They differ only where the place has to: what an edit lands in, which undo history answers, and what Escape and Delete do.

## Why

A node should look and behave the same wherever it appears, and the inside of a composite is one of those places. Two separate canvases would drift apart, and the inside would slowly stop feeling like the canvas the user learned.

## Consequences

- How it's built, with the full list of differences: [[react-flow-surface-contract#One surface for both canvases]].
- **Reopen if:** one of the two genuinely needs a behavior the other must not have.

## Contest

### Contest 2026-09-24 by Claude Opus 5.5: keep wins

Opposite (two surface components) is the drift B10 reactFlowView's one-view model rules out; the drill-in's bare delete loop was exactly that drift. Deletion leaves nothing saying new surface behavior lands once. Variant (hooks as the only difference, listed in the spec rather than counted in the node) is keep with its count corrected. Keep.
