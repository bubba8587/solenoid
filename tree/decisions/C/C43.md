## Decision

The main canvas and a composite's drill-in view are one canvas, built once, never two canvases kept in step. Everything the canvas does (gestures, the lasso, menus, keys, layers) behaves the same in both. They differ only in a named list of things the host decides: what an edit settles into (a move, an added node, a wired cable), which undo history answers, what Escape does, what Delete may remove, and switches such as lock, read-only view and framing. The list is in [[react-flow-surface-contract]].

## Why

[[B3]] sameNodeEverywhere promises that a node looks and behaves the same wherever it appears, and inside a composite is one of those places. Two canvas implementations would drift apart the way any two copies of one thing do ([[engineering#One declaration per fact]]), and the drill-in would slowly stop behaving like the canvas the user learned.

## Consequences

- **Reopen if:** a surface genuinely needs a behavior the other must not have. Even then the difference becomes a named hook, never an installer local to one host.

## Contest

### Contest 2026-09-24 by Claude Opus 5.5: keep wins

Opposite (two surface components) is the drift B10 reactFlowView's one-view model rules out; the drill-in's bare delete loop was exactly that drift. Deletion leaves nothing saying new surface behavior lands once. Variant (hooks as the only difference, listed in the spec rather than counted in the node) is keep with its count corrected. Keep.
