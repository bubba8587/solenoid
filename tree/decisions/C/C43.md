## Decision

`flow/FlowSurface.tsx` is the one canvas surface. The main canvas and the composite drill-in both render it over a `SurfaceStack` and differ only through `SurfaceHooks`. Every surface-level behavior (the gesture installers, the lasso, menus, keyboard handling and layers) is wired there once. A host supplies behavior only as named hooks: what an edit settles into (a move, an added node, a wired cable), which history answers undo, what Escape does, and the scope of the shared delete verb (what Delete may remove and how it settles, never a delete loop of its own). Every other hook is a declared switch or identity (the RF id, which layers mount, keyboard or menus off, lock, static view, framing), listed in [[react-flow-surface-contract]].

## Why

The author's call: the main canvas and the drill-in must behave the same. Two surface components would drift apart the way any two implementations of one thing do ([[C8]] declareOnce).

## Consequences

- **Reopen if:** a surface genuinely needs a behavior the other must not have. Even then the difference becomes a named hook, never an installer local to one host.

## Contest

### Contest 2026-09-24 by Claude Opus 5.5: keep wins

Opposite (two surface components) is the drift B10 reactFlowView's one-view model rules out; the drill-in's bare delete loop was exactly that drift. Deletion leaves nothing saying new surface behavior lands once. Variant (hooks as the only difference, listed in the spec rather than counted in the node) is keep with its count corrected. Keep.
