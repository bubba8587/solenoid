## Decision

A change the author makes in the vault is a work list an agent must process, never a silent edit. It reaches agents in one of three forms:

- a note dropped in the outbox folder;
- one of four tags on a leaf: `ratify`, `retire`, `contest` or `ask` (plain tags; the author ruled out nested ones);
- the author's name typed into a leaf's "ratified by" property.

An edit with none of these marks is not an instruction. Every agent session starts by working through the outbox.

## Why

[[B1]] obsidianBet makes Obsidian the author's desk, and the author edits there without a terminal. [[B8]] treeIsTheHome makes the tree the one home, so a change the author wants must land in the tree with a record of who asked for it, rather than sit as an unexplained edit. An unmarked edit can't be told apart from an agent's own unfinished work, so it carries no instruction. The inbox carries questions from agents to the author; the outbox is the other direction. Tags and a folder are the two things Obsidian makes easy, and git already knows what changed, so no watcher or plugin is needed.

## Consequences

- The author's word is the authorization. An agent acts on an outbox item and reports it, never asks again, and records the author as the one who authorized it.
- Every leaf can also be linked by its name in Obsidian, and the Obsidian views (Outbox, Unratified, Contested, Inbox, All leaves) show the work lists. The commands, and how the tool reads Obsidian's edits, are in `docs/dte.md` § Outbox and § Wikilinks.
- **Reopen if:** a DTE plugin for Obsidian processes the outbox itself, or upstream DTE ships its own channel from human to agent.
