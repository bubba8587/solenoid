## Decision

Formulas take matrices, with Excel's dynamic-array behavior, and complex numbers through the IM* functions. Nothing wider than a matrix enters a formula: Frames and Cubes belong to the Frame nodes.

## Why

[[B16]] oneFormulaSurface measures the formula surface against current Excel, and dynamic arrays are current Excel, so matrices belong in formulas. Frames and Cubes stay out for three reasons. Excel has no formula behavior for them to copy, they would compete with the Frame nodes, and handing a whole table to a formula would force loading data that a large table otherwise leaves unread on disk.

## Consequences

- Per-row math on a Frame is a computed column ([[C22]] rowFormulaRefs). Its formula sees one row's cells and whole columns as lists, never the Frame itself.
- Matrices and complex numbers never reach the open-source function library the formula bar falls back on, which would answer them wrongly ([[formula-language#The dispatch ladder]]).
- When shapes don't match, values line up the way Excel's dynamic arrays do ([[formula-language#Broadcasting]]). Uneven element-wise math pads with blanks, and functions that build a shape pad with `#N/A` ([[C48]] appendLadder). A List counts as one row.
- A request to widen Expression is answered with a Composite instead.
- How it's built: [[formula-language]].
- **Reopen if:** broadcasting diverges from Excel in ways users hit. A new cap would be a new decision, not a silent revert. Nothing at the formula level reopens Frames or Cubes.
