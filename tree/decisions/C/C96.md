## Decision

A chart's options are typed as a short list like `marker=o; fontsize=12`, using the option names matplotlib uses (`marker`, `fontsize`, `linestyle` and so on) rather than names of our own. An option the app doesn't know is ignored, so the chart still draws.

## Why

People who style charts in text already know one vocabulary, and it is matplotlib's; a new one would have to be learned for no gain.

## Consequences

- Only a concept matplotlib lacks gets a name of our own. How it's built, and every key: [[chart-figures#The options string]].
- **Reopen if:** the options move to separate fields with their own controls, which would make the text a derived view.

## Contest

### Contest 2026-09-26 by Claude Opus 5.5: keep wins

Keep. Against B14 oneDesignSystem: one borrowed vocabulary is one rulebook for every chart; the opposite (names of our own) adds a second dialect to learn and document, and deletion leaves each new key's name to taste, which is how two figures drift to two spellings of one option. Scoped against the XY column-mapping keys (x, y, s, c, linestyle, aspect, xlim): each has a matplotlib/pandas name, only the point-label and split-by keys need names of our own.
