## Decision

Mathlib, the machine-checked mathematics library of the Lean prover, is where Solenoid takes the laws its answers keep, and Solenoid relies on it wherever a law is useful and relevant: matrices (a determinant and an inverse at every scale), totals and aggregates (order doesn't matter, which subtotals add up), points and differences (dates and temperature readings), the type order behind which sockets connect and how types settle, equation solving (a common factor changes no solution), and units and rounding. A spec that states such a law cites the mathlib declaration that proves it, and the test that holds the law cites it too.

## Why

A law proved by a machine is a better source than our own arithmetic: citing it says where a truth came from, and the law becomes a one-line test that catches a wrong answer before a user does. Mathlib works over exact numbers and makes some choices only for proofs (dividing by zero gives 0), so Solenoid borrows its laws, not its conventions, and never ships it.

## Consequences

- Mathlib is never a dependency and its vocabulary stays out of the app's words; it lives in specs and tests.
- A law over exact numbers holds for floating point only to a tolerance, and a test says which; where floating point breaks a law outright (a product's last digits follow the order of its values), the spec names the exception.
- **Reopen if:** a law Solenoid needs has no mathlib home, or a mathlib convention would be the better answer for users.
