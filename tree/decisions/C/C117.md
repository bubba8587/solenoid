## Decision

Text read as a number (VALUE, Cast, a typed cell, a pasted value) is read in the US form only: `.` is the decimal point and `,` groups thousands. A European form such as `1.234,5` is refused as not a number, never reread as a different one.

## Why

[[A5]] excelParity reads numbers the way US Excel does, and one fixed reading makes the same text mean the same number on every machine, as [[C59]] byteStringOrder does for sorting. Guessing a locale would let `1.234` mean one thousand two hundred and thirty-four in one place and one point two three four in another. A refusal is visible; a silent misreading (`1.234,5` as 1.2345) is not.

## Consequences

- A comma anywhere before the decimal point is a grouping mark (`1,5` is 15, as in US Excel); a comma after it makes the text not a number.
- A currency sign other than `$` is not stripped (`€5` is not a number).
- **Reopen if:** users outside the US need their own number form, which would mean a per-document locale setting rather than a guess per value.
