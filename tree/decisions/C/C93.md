## Decision

A gesture behaves according to what is touching the screen (a finger, a mouse or a pen), never according to what kind of device it is. With a finger, dragging an unselected node or group pans the canvas, and dragging a selected one moves it (tap, then drag). A mouse or a pen selects and drags in one motion. A pen counts as a precise pointer: it never takes part in a pinch, and there is no palm rejection.

## Why

[[B14]] oneDesignSystem: the canvas answers the hand the same way everywhere, so it goes by what touches the screen, which makes a tablet running the desktop layout behave exactly like a phone under a finger. A busy graph leaves no blank space to pan from, so under a finger any part of an unselected node that isn't a control is somewhere to pan from. Solenoid is a precise editor, not a drawing surface, so nobody rests a palm on it and palm rejection isn't worth its cost. These are the author's calls.

## Consequences

- Only buttons and similar controls, sockets and the resize grip stop a finger pan; text fields do not (a tap focuses one, a drag pans).
- A tap still selects.
- In touch-select mode, each tap adds a node to the selection or removes it, and a finger drag on an unselected node no longer pans.
- How it's built: the pointer-gestures spec.
- **Reopen if:** a drawing-style surface lands in the app; it would want the shape and pressure of each touch, not just whether a pen is in use.
