## Decision

A pack node presents one face (declared input sockets with restriction metadata and defaults, declared outputs, a locked body) and comes in two builds. A simple node is one compiled expression, optionally with a variant dropdown. A composite node is an encapsulated subgraph with a declared boundary, built as a real subgraph (`CompositeNode`), not as a Group variant. The simple node is the degenerate, single-node case of the composite.

## Why

A Group is the opposite of a pack node on every axis: it has no sockets and no `data()`, its membership is spatial or hybrid, and its boundary is inferred, while a pack node has a declared contract and computes. Treating the simple node as a one-node composite means that growing a pack node from simple to composite changes nothing about how packs appear, how restrictions attach, or how errors surface.

## Consequences

- A simple pack node never builds boolean-gate or clamp sub-nodes to enforce a restriction; a restriction is data.
- Members run in a private `NodeEditor` and `DataflowEngine`; internals never leak into the outer engine or its cache.
