## Decision

A pack node built from several nodes is a real composite node with fixed inputs and outputs, never a kind of Group. A pack node built from one formula is treated as a composite with one node inside, not as a different thing, so both builds look the same from outside: named inputs with their limits and defaults, named outputs, and a locked inside.

## Why

[[B15]] leanCore makes a pack a promise of named inputs and outputs built on the core's toolkit, and the composite is the toolkit's way of packaging several nodes behind such a promise. A Group is the opposite in every way: it has no inputs or outputs and computes nothing, what it holds depends on where things sit on the canvas, and its edge is worked out rather than declared. Treating a one-formula node as a one-node composite means that growing a pack node from simple to composite changes nothing about how packs appear, how input limits attach, or how errors show.

## Consequences

- A simple pack node never hides extra nodes inside to enforce an input's limit; a limit is a setting on the input.
- The nodes inside a composite are computed on their own and never mix with the outer document's calculation. How: [[composite-nodes]].
