## Decision

**MUST:** a rule or decision is author-ruled if and only if the author has read it in a session and marked it themselves. In the tree that mark is `ratified_by`, set on the author's word in that session. Nothing else confers it: not something the author said in the past, not a recorded decision, not a quote in a node's Why, and not an agent's confidence about what the author meant.

## Why

[[B9]] rulesWithoutMemory needs the author's actual rulings to be told apart from agents' inferences, and ratification in the tree is exactly that mark ([[B8]] treeIsTheHome). The author, in their own words: "99% of what is in this codebase is your assumptions and references you built for yourself unless you recorded that I told you something specific… even if I said something in the past — it's not an author-ruled rule as of right now."

## Consequences

- A leaf the author has not ratified binds only as the working agent's inference or default, and is open to question on those terms.
- The grades from the retired `rules.md` carry over. An inferred rule keeps its forcing incident in its Why. A default rule (a preventive judgment with no incident behind it, the thinnest ice) has confidence medium and is the first candidate for an enforcing incident or for deletion. The grade records where a rule came from, not what it is worth.
- To authorize, the author walks `python3 tools/dte.py tree` and ratifies leaf by leaf, or tells an agent to run `ratify`. To amend, the author dictates the amendment first. To reject, the leaf is superseded or reverted in the same sitting.
