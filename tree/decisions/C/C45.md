## Decision

Checking whether two texts are equal ignores case, as Excel's `=` does: equality, lookups, criteria and the Filter's text tests all treat "Apple" and "apple" as the same. EXACT and the "Match case" option compare case-sensitively. Telling rows apart respects case instead: join keys, grouping and Distinct keep "Apple" and "apple" separate.

## Why

Excel's `=` ignores case, so comparisons here do too. Keys are a different job: merging "Apple" and "apple" into one group or one match would destroy a distinction that was in the data, and nothing on screen would show it.

## Consequences

- Sorting goes by character code, so it respects case ([[C59]] byteStringOrder).
- How it's built: [[formula-language]] (*Scalar operators*) and [[frame-verbs]].
- **Reopen if:** people more often want "Apple" and "apple" joined or grouped together than kept apart.
