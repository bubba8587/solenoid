## Decision

Every equality test and match ignores case: `=` and `<>`, the Comparison node's equality ops, lookup matches and the Filter's text ops. EXACT and the "Match case" option are the way to compare case-sensitively. Every identity operation respects case instead: Join keys, GROUPBY keys and Distinct. Ordering (`<`, `>`, sorts) goes by character code, so it respects case too ([[C59]] byteStringOrder).

## Why

[[B16]] oneFormulaSurface measures answers against Excel, and Excel's `=` ignores case, so comparisons do too. Keys are a different job. Merging "Apple" and "apple" into one group or one join match destroys a distinction that was in the data, and nothing on screen would show it happened.

## Consequences

- A per-verb option to ignore case in keys would be an addition inside this rule, not a change to it.
