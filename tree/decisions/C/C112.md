## Decision

Every layout action ends by pulling apart anything that overlaps, anywhere on the canvas. Align is a layout action, and so is opening a collapsed card. A drag isn't: things stay where you put them. Neither is a card growing with its live content, since that can happen on any recalculation; the next layout action separates it.

## Why

Overlapping nodes look broken, and nobody overlaps them on purpose.

## Consequences

- How it's built: [[group-expand-push#LayoutFx]].
- *Exception:* a paste lands at the pointer with a slight offset and may cover a card; that's intended, since where you paste is where you want it. **Removed by:** nothing; a paste is placed by hand.
- *Unenforced:* Align and opening a collapsed card don't run the overlap cleanup yet (`docs/backlog.md` § Leaves the code breaks).
- **Reopen if:** a kind of overlap is wanted on purpose, such as a Note laid over a node.

## Contest

### Contest 2026-09-24 by Claude Opus 5.5: keep wins

Opposite (overlaps allowed, or best-effort) is what B14 oneDesignSystem rules out: an overlap reads as a broken canvas. Deletion leaves no node owning the final pass, which is how two exemptions crept back in. Variant (exempt overlaps the user made before the op) was tried and broke the guarantee twice. Keep.
