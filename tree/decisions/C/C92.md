## Decision

Pinch listens in the capture phase on the surface wrapper and cannot be vetoed; pan and node drag listen in the bubble phase and can be. A finger is any contact whose `pointerType` is not `mouse` or `pen` (a missing type counts as touch), counted once at window capture in `pointerGesture.ts`. Everything that yields to a pinch reads that count and never counts raw pointers itself.

## Why

React Flow's node drag and pane pan bind in the bubble phase on their own elements, so a capture listener that takes two-touch moves always runs before them. No component's `stopPropagation` can hide a finger from the zoom, and no call site has to remember not to. The opposite design, counting fingers in bubble as rete's stock Zoom did, let any swallowed `pointerdown` kill the pinch for the whole gesture. A control that wants the pointer for itself (a slider, dial or text field) can still veto the pan and the drag by stopping the event; only the zoom is unstoppable.

## Consequences

- Touch events, not pointer events, drive the pinch, because only touch events reliably list every finger.
- A pinch never selects. When the gesture ends, a short capture-phase click guard swallows the first finger's click. The click is prevented rather than rolled back, because a visible correction reads as a glitch.
- **Reopen if:** React Flow moves its handlers to the capture phase, which would break the ordering guarantee.
