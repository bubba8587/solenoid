## Decision

A two-finger pinch always zooms the canvas, whatever it starts on: no node or control can stop it. Panning and dragging a node, by contrast, give way to a control that wants the pointer for itself (a slider, a dial, a text field). Any touch that is not a mouse or a pen counts as a finger, and everything that gives way to a pinch counts fingers the same way. How the listeners are wired is in [[pointer-gestures]].

## Why

[[B14]] oneDesignSystem: the canvas answers the hand the same way everywhere. A pinch that something under the fingers could swallow would zoom on one part of the canvas and not another, with nothing to show why, and a pinch that died partway would jerk the view. A control does need the pointer for itself, so pan and drag give way to it; only the zoom is unstoppable.

## Consequences

- Touch events, not pointer events, drive the pinch, because only touch events reliably list every finger.
- A pinch never selects. When the gesture ends, a short capture-phase click guard swallows the first finger's click. The click is prevented rather than rolled back, because a visible correction reads as a glitch.
- **Reopen if:** React Flow moves its handlers to the capture phase, which would break the ordering guarantee.
