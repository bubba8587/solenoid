## Decision

A Weights table may give a criterion an At least or At most. An option whose value falls outside either is ruled out before anything is weighed: it isn't scored, doesn't shape the scaling of the rest, and is listed after the ranking with the criteria that ruled it out. A missing value never rules an option out. A Choice Ladder applies the same screen, and its Options may carry Must and Never columns: a Must is taken before anything else, a Never is never taken, and Musts that can't all fit, exclude each other, or are ruled out themselves are an error naming the clash. A One of column groups options so at most one per group is taken.

## Why

People rule options out before they weigh what's left: an apartment over budget isn't a cheap apartment scored down, it's not on the list. Scoring it down lets a high score elsewhere buy back a hard limit, and leaving it in the scaling bends every other option's score. A promised hire or a withdrawn candidate is the same kind of fact for a set of picks.

## Consequences

- How it's built: [[frame-verbs#Decision Matrix and Decision Sensitivity]] and [[frame-verbs#Choice Ladder]].
- A weight of 0 makes a criterion a screen only, and a row that sets a limit with its weight left blank is one.
- **Reopen if:** someone needs a soft limit, a penalty that grows past a threshold instead of a cutoff.
