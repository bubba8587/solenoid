## Decision

In the Decision Matrix, each Breakdown column shows how much one criterion added to or took away from an option's score, so the columns add up to the Score, and a criterion with a negative weight shows as the penalty it is. Options are ranked on the score as shown (rounded to 4 decimal places), so the displayed score and the rank can never disagree. Both decision nodes scale each criterion by dividing by its largest value unless told otherwise. On an exact tie, Decision Sensitivity names every first-place option as the Winner ("A = B"), and its Margin is 0 exactly when there is a tie. Sensitivity answers `#VALUE!` when no row of its Scenarios table names a criterion.

## Why

Each default removes a plausible wrong reading ([[A6]] divergeByDesign). Bare normalized values read backwards under a negative weight: the priciest option showed Price 1.0 while taking the largest penalty. Unscaled values silently mislead the moment criteria use different scales (dollars beside a 1 to 5 rating), and comparing unlike criteria is the node's whole reason to exist. And when no Scenarios row matches a criterion, every weight falls back to 1, so every scenario comes out identical: the trap a renamed criterion springs.
