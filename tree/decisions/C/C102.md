## Decision

A blank cell in a bordered grid is filled by straight-line interpolation from the nearest square of known values, the answer MATLAB and SciPy give. Only with Forecast on (the default) does the grid also fit a smooth surface through every known value, filling the gaps no square covers and extending the trend past the edges. With Forecast off it never guesses past its data: a lookup table stays a lookup table, and a cell no square encloses stays blank.

## Why

[[B17]] typedValueModel says nothing is made up behind the user's back. A filled cell inside the known boxes is a reading of the data, so it gives the answer MATLAB and SciPy give, the tools people check it against since Excel has none. A value past the data is a guess, a different promise, so it appears only when Forecast is switched on, never as a side effect.

## Consequences

- How the fill works (the two passes, how far the search for a square widens and why, when a square defers to the surface, the fallback when too few points are known): [[bordered-grid-fill]]; `stats.test.ts` pins it.
- **Reopen if:** a user needs a trend that isn't a straight line, or grids grow past what the capped search handles.
