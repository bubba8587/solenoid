## Decision

A blank cell in a bordered grid fills in two passes: first true bilinear interpolation from the nearest box whose four corners are all known (the bracket widens past blank corners, capped at four lines), then, only with `forecast` on (the default), a smooth surface through every known point (thin-plate spline, a plane when degenerate) fills what remains and extrapolates a linear edge trend. Forecast off never extrapolates: a lookup table stays a lookup table. A box with data between its samples is CONTESTED and defers to the surface (author ruling 2026-07-16).

## Why

Bilinear from the nearest known box is what MATLAB's `interp2` and SciPy's `RegularGridInterpolator` answer, so a user checking a filled cell gets the number they expect. Extrapolation is a different promise, so it is a named switch rather than a side effect. The widening cap is load-bearing: uncapped, scattered data rejects every candidate box and the search goes O(lines⁴) per cell. The contested rule exists because of the sine-diagonal case, where the only all-known box was the grid's four zero corners and every blank filled flat zero with the whole diagonal inside it.

## Consequences

- The passes, the cap, the contested test and the degenerate fallback are [[bordered-grid-fill]]; `stats.test.ts` pins them.
- **Reopen if:** a user needs a non-linear extrapolation trend, or the grid grows past what O(lines⁴) at cap 4 tolerates.
