## Decision

Diagonal and straight cable modes share one router, `routeWalk`, which enumerates walks and is parameterized by the compass division. Its constraints hold by construction, with no fallback to a sharper turn: a rigid exit stub and entry stub along each socket's direction, every other segment on a compass heading, and every turn exactly one compass step. It picks the shortest solvable walk overall, by length first; turn count and rotation preference only break exact ties. Spline mode is a single cubic whose tangents match the sockets exactly.

## Why

The heuristic routers this replaced (`getDiagonalPath`, `getSmoothStepPath`, `withAngleLeads`) needed fallbacks, and the fallbacks produced kinks. Any selection that ranks turn count ahead of length turns the handoff from one route to the next into a visible jump. A walk becomes feasible at exactly the length of its own wider extension, which was already competing, so ranking by length first makes the handoff seamless.

## Consequences

- Property tests machine-check the invariants and the continuity under simulated sub-pixel drags. The remaining discontinuities (collapse when sockets nearly touch, swaps between equal-length walks) are inherent and listed in [[cable-rendering-knobs]].
- The renderer's dedup epsilon stays well under a pixel, or it deletes real vertices.
- **Reopen if:** cables need obstacle avoidance, which no walk family provides.
