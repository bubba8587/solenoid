## Decision

The Choice Ladder node turns a Decision Matrix ranking into the order to do things in when you can't do them all. A limit can be counted, as a cap or a floor (a budget, at least two vegetarian places), or rough with no total (political capital), and options can require or exclude one another. Where two options sit close in its order (their Decision Matrix scores, or with a rough cost their score for what it costs) and the choice changes which options end up taken, it forks, the flow splitting between them, and the user picks; the node remembers the pick and keeps the side passed over in view.

## Why

Whatever the constraint, the question is what to do next with what's left, so the answer is an order; a rough cost still makes a favorite that eats everything worth less. Close scores are where the person, not the weights, should decide, and only where deciding changes the outcome.
