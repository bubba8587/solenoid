## Decision

**MUST:** a module imported by the formula path doesn't pull in rete, the socket lattice or the frame model.

**MUST (every sibling, including the next one):** the rule governs every module in the [[C17]] shareImpl seam, not just the ones listed there. A new shared module is created under this rule: check a sibling's header before writing it, and state the constraint in its own header the way `dateSerial.ts`, `convertUnits.ts` and `listOps.ts` do. `frame.ts` and `unitColumn.ts` are the two easy traps. Both look like value-layer modules and both reach rete (`frame.ts` → `sockets.ts`; `unitColumn.ts` → `unitBridge.ts` → `formatAnnotationStore.ts` → `nodes/date.ts`).

**MUST (what to extract):** share only what both surfaces can hold. A node kernel that also handles Frames or Cubes doesn't move whole, since a formula holds neither ([[D26]] hideMatrixFromVendor). That half stays node-side and calls the shared core (`indexAccess.ts` plus the frame and cube branch in `nodes/list.ts` is the pattern), and a needed dirty helper arrives as an argument instead of an import (`tagFrameCellUnit` into `indexInto`).

## Why

The headless formula path (`run-graph`, the evaluator) shouldn't load the editor. The rule was broken three times while it was only a convention: `excelFunctions` reached rete through `nodes/date.ts` and `nodes/convert.ts` until the serial helpers and unit table were extracted, and a new sibling, `indexAccess.ts`, extracted the INDEX node's `data()` whole, importing `frame.ts`. That last one is why the rule names the moment a sibling is created and what not to extract.
