## Decision

**MUST:** a catalog entry's rendered `label` carries only what a reader needs to identify and pick it. Every alternate spelling a user might type, Excel function names first, is declared in a searched but unrendered field (`keywords`, or `CATALOG_TO_EXCEL` for a whole leaf), never appended to the label to make it findable.

## Why

Findability and legibility are separate jobs, and the search layer already separates them: `scoreLeaf` scores `keywords` at full field weight, so moving a name out of the label costs nothing and can improve its rank. Stuffing the label pays twice. The name is duplicated against the declaration it came from ([[C8]] declareOnce), and the Add menu sizes every row to the widest one, so one overlong label is a defect in the whole menu. When the Distribution ops each carried their full dotted Excel list, one row measured 630px against a 94px median and stretched the panel from 174px to 525px on the first keystroke. The mechanism is in `specs/add-menu.md`.

## Consequences

*Exception:* a parenthetical that tells apart two entries sharing a name stays in the label ("T.TEST (equal var)" vs "T.TEST (Welch)", "TAKE (table)", "DATE (Build)"), and so does a bare acronym that is the name people know ("Growth Rate (CAGR)"). What's barred is an Excel function spelling: dotted, or several separated by slashes. Removed when the menu gains per-row truncation; then the length pressure goes, and only [[C8]] declareOnce still argues.
