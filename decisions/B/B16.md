## Decision

The formula surface and the node canvas are ONE function set: every node is an Excel-named function and every function is reachable as a node, from one implementation. Parity is measured against current Excel, on every surface at once (formula bar, Add menu, Function Reference, catalog descriptions), and where an Excel quirk and cross-engine consistency conflict, consistency wins and the divergence is named.

## Why

[[A5]] excelParity is the promise; this is the shape that keeps it. Two implementations of one function drift, and a name that differs between the formula bar and the card is a trap of the same kind as a wrong answer. Measuring against current Excel (not 2003's aliases) is what lets a user's knowledge transfer without a legacy layer, and naming every divergence is what lets A5 and [[A6]] divergeByDesign coexist.

## Consequences

- The rule families here: one implementation ([[C17]] shareImpl), naming ([[C51]] formulaNaming, [[C18]] uniqueNameMap, [[C14]] currentExcelParity), arguments ([[C20]] wholeArrayArgs, [[C21]] matchNodeLimits, [[C22]] rowFormulaRefs, [[C80]] blankArgIsExcelBlank, [[C15]] matricesInFormulas, [[C50]] lambdaBindsByName), answers ([[C45]] excelComparisons, [[C46]] consistencyOverQuirks), and recalculation ([[C23]] calcModes).
- A new function lands on both surfaces in one change or on neither.
- **Reopen if:** a surface is wanted that deliberately exposes fewer functions than the other (a "simple mode"); it is then a filter over the one set, never a second set.
