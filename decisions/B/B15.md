## Decision

Solenoid ships a lean core and makes most node families optional add-ons (packs); each pack declares whether it starts active (`defaultActive`). The shared toolkit every pack builds on stays in the core, always: the canvas and the calculation engine, the unit system, socket types and the socket legend, the formula compiler that powers the Expression node, the basic look of a node, and save/load.

## Why

A1: someone opening Solenoid for the first time should see a clean, simple tool, not a wall of two hundred specialist nodes they will never use. The toolkit is the core's promise to every pack (build on these and we will not break you): if two packs could disagree on units or sockets, graphs would stop fitting together, and if packs could reach past the toolkit into the core's internals, the core could never change again without breaking them.

## Consequences

- **Where:** `packs.ts` (registry + activation), `fcExtensions.ts`; the authoring guide is `pack-architecture.md`.
- Open pack work (more packs, distribution and dependencies, port aliasing) lives in the backlog.

## Alternatives considered

### Contest 2026-09-15 by Claude Opus 5: keep wins

Rubric: A1.
- keep: lean core, optional packs, a fixed shared toolkit. Cost: pack registry, activation store, a formula-pack format; the toolkit is frozen as a promise.
- opposite: every node family in the core, always visible. The Add menu and Reference grow to hundreds of specialist nodes; the scratchpad end of A1 drowns.
- deletion: A1 alone does not say where a specialist node family belongs or what packs may rely on; each new family re-argues it.
- variant: packs may ship their own units or sockets. Two packs disagreeing on units breaks wiring between them, and the flagship unit feature fractures.
Verdict: keep.
