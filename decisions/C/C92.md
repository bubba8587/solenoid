## Decision

Pinch listens in CAPTURE on the surface wrapper and cannot be vetoed; pan and node drag listen in BUBBLE and can be. A finger is any contact whose `pointerType` is not `mouse` or `pen` (absent counts as touch), counted once at window capture (`pointerGesture.ts`); everything that yields to a pinch reads that census and never counts raw pointers.

## Why

React Flow's node drag and pane pan bind in bubble on their elements, so a capture listener that consumes two-touch moves out-prioritises them positionally: no component's `stopPropagation` can hide a finger from the zoom, and no call site has to remember not to. The opposite (count fingers in bubble, as rete's stock Zoom did) let any swallowed pointerdown kill the pinch for the whole gesture. A control that wants the pointer for itself (slider, dial, text field) can still veto the pan and the drag by stopping the event; only the zoom is unstoppable.

## Consequences

- Touch events, not pointer events, drive the pinch: multi-finger input is only reliably enumerable there.
- A pinch never selects: gesture end arms a short capture-phase click guard that swallows finger 1's click, prevented rather than rolled back (a visible correction reads as a glitch).
- **Reopen if:** React Flow moves its handlers to capture, which would break the positional guarantee.
