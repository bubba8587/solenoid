## Decision

Every equality test and match ignores case: `=` and `<>`, the Comparison node's equality ops, lookup matches and the Filter's text ops. Ordering (`<`, `>`, sorts) is code-unit order and so case-sensitive ([[C59]] byteStringOrder). EXACT and the "Match case" option are the way to compare case-sensitively. Every identity operation is case-sensitive: Join keys, Group By keys and Distinct. Error cells are the one place the two dedupers differ: list UNIQUE never merges error cells, while Frame Distinct merges them by error code.

## Why

[[A5]]: Excel's `=` ignores case, so comparisons do too. Keys are a different job. Merging "Apple" and "apple" into one group or one join match destroys a distinction that was in the data, and nothing on screen would show it happened.

## Consequences

- A per-verb option to ignore case in keys would be an addition inside this rule, not a change to it.
