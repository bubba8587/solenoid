## Decision

**MUST:** a function callable from both a node and a formula has exactly one implementation, in a rete-free module (`nodes/listOps.ts`, `textOps.ts`, `financeOps.ts`, `mathUtils.ts`, `dateSerial.ts`, `convertUnits.ts`, `nodes/matrixOps.ts`, `nodes/indexAccess.ts`, `cxValue.ts`). Both callers delegate to it.

**MUST (capability parity, the author's standing order):** the node exposes everything the formula surface can do for that function. No argument, mode or return shape reachable from the formula may be unreachable from the node; the node carries a socket, an op dropdown or a control for each. Diverging from Excel or Formula.js is a judgement call, documented in the function's `nodeExcel` note. Our own two surfaces disagreeing is a defect and never ships.

- A node that dispatches through `resolveExcelFunction` passes the function's full argument list.
- A node with its own computation covers every mode the registration does. Prefer routing both surfaces through one shared kernel so they can't drift; the REGEX ops compose `regexApply`, `replaceNth` and `regexGroups` on both sides.

## Why

The two surfaces drifted for exactly as long as they were two implementations.

## Consequences

- *Exceptions:* `SHUFFLE` can't assert that node equals formula, because it is volatile and the two surfaces run different volatility clocks on purpose: the node holds its sort keys until the next recalc, and a formula redraws on every evaluation. The permutation is still one implementation (`shuffleList` takes its keys as an argument), and the test asserts a permutation plus real variation instead of equality. **Removed by:** nothing; this is what volatile means. Any future volatile function follows the same split.
- A specific argument may be a sanctioned shortfall with a recorded reason: an Excel argument that is a cell-grid concept, or one the formula surface also lacks.
- **Reopen if:** never as a whole (a standing order).
