## Decision

A palette can replace App.css's neutral ramp: canvas, window, three surfaces, three borders, four inks, per theme mode (`BUILTIN_CHROME`). It is PARALLEL to the slot map rather than more slots. The remaining literal-valued neutrals (panel/overlay fills, overlay border, button hover, gauge track, selected cable, wordmark, light-theme shadows) DERIVE from those 13 by fixed mixes.

All or nothing per palette: `Default` alone authors none (it IS App.css); every other built-in authors a COMPLETE ramp in both modes. A ramp may recolor the workbench but not renege on its STRUCTURE. A TINTED ramp follows the live accent. CONTRAST (WCAG AA 4.5:1 ink on card and field) is scoped to two palettes: `Default` and `Colorblind-safe`.

## Why

Nothing stores a neutral on a node, `resolveColor` never returns one, and folding neutrals into the slot map would make every slot consumer (chart series, swatch grid, height ramp, doc overrides) skip them. The derived neutrals are calibrated by running the DEFAULT ramp through the mixes and matching App.css's own hand-tuned literals, so an author moves one ramp, and a step that reproduces the tuned values also behaves on a ramp nobody has eyeballed.

A partial ramp writes its keys and derives nothing, landing the app in a mix of authored neutrals and stylesheet ones; a Default copy would be two homes for one truth. Tinted-ramp rotation runs in OKLCh because the first cut rotated in HSL, whose saturation is hue-anisotropic, and the workbench came out "washed in the color" (author), up to 2× the authored perceived chroma on Orchard's dark ground. Contrast is scoped because Default is the experience nobody chose and Colorblind-safe's brief is legibility, while the rest are aesthetic opt-ins whose value is fidelity to a look (Solarized sits near 3:1 by design).

## Consequences

- `appTheme` CLEARS every chrome var per apply, since an inline property beats the stylesheet and a stale one would strand cream chrome under Default. The custom palette's ramp is always complete (seeded from `DEFAULT_CHROME`, a hand-held mirror of App.css).
- Structure holds for every ramp, the App.css baseline included (a rule Default fails is a wrong rule): canvas darker than card; dot legible on canvas without shouting; the field brightest in light and a recess in dark; hover fill stepping toward the ink; three border tiers stepping outward; four ink tiers stepping down in contrast.
- Tinted ramps (2026-08-18): Orchard and Blueprint declare their home accent (`CHROME_HOME`: green / blue); appTheme rotates the whole ramp by the hue delta to the selected accent in OKLCh, holding every key's chroma AND its WCAG relative luminance, so the authored ramp reappears byte-identical at the home accent. Chroma may only DROP in transit, never inflate. This does NOT reopen the socket-sibling HSV rule (DESIGN.md §Tertiary), whose steps stay near their own hue. Adaptation is per-palette by declaration: Solarized, Colorblind-safe, Equinox, Muted and Custom do not rotate. An accent below the OKLCh chroma floor leaves the ramp authored.
- Contrast scope narrowed 2026-08-09 from the original all-palettes rule; structure still applies to all.
- **Where:** `palette.ts` (`BUILTIN_CHROME` / `DEFAULT_CHROME` / `chromeCssVars` / `CHROME_HOME` / `adaptChrome`), `appTheme.ts` `apply`, `PaletteEditor.tsx`, `palette.test.ts` §§ chrome ramp, chrome ramp structure, accent-adaptive chrome, chromeCssVars (`AA_PALETTES` is the scope).
- **Reopen if:** chrome outgrows flat colors (a texture, a vignette, a per-doc ramp); that wants its own model. If a palette other than these two ever becomes a default for anyone, it joins `AA_PALETTES` that day.
